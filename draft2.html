Orbis · concept draft 2 · from rounds 1 to 3

The planet fights back

You land on a planet that is alive, and it treats you as an infection. You build a fortified settlement while it answers with swarms, giant creatures and plants, storms and deadly nights. You spread across the globe one region at a time and choose what to study: the exotic material, to build a war machine; the wildlife, to bond with the planet; or the ruins of the lost civilisation, to take what they left. Then the rival lands.

00

Why are we here?

The reason has to explain why this planet and not an easier one. The strongest answer is that the planet is hard because it is rich: whatever makes it worth having is also what makes it fight.

Why this works in play, not just in the story

  • Every next region is a choice: safe and poor, or rich and angry. That is the campaign's risk and reward.
  • The danger has a cause you can learn and plan around, so it feels like a place rather than random spawns.
  • It explains the rival: once word of what you found gets out, someone else comes for it.
  • It sets up the ending: whatever made the planet rich and angry is what the lost civilisation left behind.

Four reasons to come, and why each picks the hard planet

The prize

Something here exists nowhere else: an exotic material, or the lost civilisation's technology. The company pays for the trip.

Why the hard planet: the easy ones have nothing worth the trip.

The exodus

Home is failing and this is the only world in reach of the ships.

Why the hard planet: there was no other choice.

The claim

A charter race: whoever holds the planet when the claim window closes owns it.

Why the hard planet: it's the one worth racing for, and the rival is coming.

The call

A signal from the ruins brought you here. Something wanted you to come.

Why the hard planet: you were invited, and you don't know why yet.

Three layers of why

Each layer is true at a different point in the campaign. They fit your Q5 sides and the settlement you keep safe.

At the startWhat the company says: the prize. They fund the military landing to secure it. This is your briefing.
UnderneathWhat the colonists want: a way out. Home is failing and they signed on for a new one. The settlement is their home, not a mine, which is why keeping it safe matters.
Mid-campaignWhat the ruins reveal: the truth. The prize is what the lost civilisation was built on, and the planet's fury is connected to it: a defence they built, or the planet's answer to being mined. The company knew more than it said.

Why all four works

  • It's one cause, not four McGuffins. The core's energy rises and crystallises in the rock; the life that feeds on it grows huge; the lost civilisation built on it. Learn one, and the others make sense.
  • It explains every threat: storms (the energy), megafauna and swarms (the life), guardians (the lost tech), all strongest where the ground is richest.
  • It shapes the tech tree: the material, the life and the ruins are three branches, each with its own story, and all three end at the core, which is what lets you reshape the planet.
  • The life is studied, not sold. Specimens from fights and nests go to the lab, and each study pays off three ways (below).
  • It feeds the layers of why: the company sells the material; the truth in the ruins is what the core really is.
  • The one risk: if the four feel separate, the story blurs. Keep one name for the substance and show the link early.

Studying the life

Fighting a species teaches you how to fight it better. That is a reason to go into the nests, not just a cost.

02

The loop of play

Seven steps around a circle. Every lap the threats get bigger and so do your weapons.

What each step asks of you

  • Land and build: a landing period with nothing attacking. Place the settlement, the first walls, the power.
  • Flow: extractors bring in mass, generators energy, homes bring people. Spend faster than you earn and everything slows.
  • Scout: find the nests, the megafauna's paths, the ruins, the rich ground.
  • Take: clear a region's nests. That is where the frontier fighting happens.
  • Fortify: a region you fortify properly looks after itself (section 05).
  • Explore: dig the ruins for technology, story and caches. Digging may wake the guardians.
  • Tech up: new tiers bring bigger weapons, and the planet answers with bigger threats.
03

Who's on the planet, and when

One deep playable side. The threats arrive in order: the natives first, the machines when you dig, a rival when you're established.

04

The living planet

The natives aren't animals defending a territory. The planet is one living thing, and everything that attacks you is part of it: its immune system, answering an infection.

Giants: not just one sandworm

Your Q8 note: many species, and plant life too. A first sketch:

  • roamer Hunters that roam: cross regions, follow noise and vibration. Heavy industry draws them.
  • guardian Guardians of places: sit on the richest ground, nests and ruins. A boss fight to take the region.
  • lure Can be lured or used: lead one into an enemy; the wildlife branch can call them (planet-shaping, section 09).

Megaflora: the plants fight too

  • Creeping forest: grows toward your walls and slowly swallows what it reaches. Burn it back.
  • Spore towers: breathe out the mist that blocks orbit. Clearing the mist means taking them down.
  • Snare plants: living walls the swarms build their nests behind.
  • Bloom: a giant flower that heals and spurs the swarm near it. Kill it first.

Why it matters

  • Where you go sets how hard it is: a polar region is a long fight in the dark; an equatorial one gives you time to build.
  • Pushing back the dark (section 09) matters most where the nights are longest.
  • Orbis is most of the way there: the clock already moves the sun over the real planet, so day length by latitude is a small step on from it.

Why the poles are the endgame

  • The dark feeds the planet. In the long polar night the swarms breed and the megaflora spreads unchecked, so the nests there are the oldest and biggest.
  • And it holds the prize. The richest seams and the deepest ruins, and the core's energy closest to the surface: the aurora is it breaking through.
  • The campaign has a shape: land near the equator, push toward a pole. Every step colder, darker, richer.
  • Pushing back the dark is what makes it possible. Floodlights won't hold a polar night; you need scrubbers, uplinks and mirrors (section 09).
  • Two poles: the rival could land at the other one, and race you to it.
05

One planet, region by region

Every region has a status. Your attention stays on the frontier; fortifying a region is how you earn the right to stop worrying about it.

How it ends: three ways to win

Not escape. Each ending matches one of the three layers of why (section 00).

  • Dominate Hold and fortify every region. No special tech or understanding needed, just strength. The planet never stops fighting; you outlast it. The hardest fight.
  • Inherit Dig to the bottom of the ruins and face what was left there. Win, and its power is yours. Then choose: wipe out the natives, or hold them back for good.
  • Stabilise Calm the core instead of fighting it. "Make peace" felt wrong because you can't negotiate with an ecosystem. But the fury comes from the core's energy, and your mining stirs it up. Study the life, reach the core, and settle it: the swarms thin and the storms ease. You get there by bonding with the planet: your people and machines blend with its life until it stops treating you as an infection.

What makes Stabilise work: one meter

The planet's agitation. Mining, terraforming and digging raise it; it drives how big the waves and storms get. Dominate ignores it and fights through; Stabilise has to keep it down, which means mining less and holding more. The same meter also makes "richer ground, angrier planet" a rule you can see.

06

Anatomy of a base

Rings, from the people at the heart to the border you claim. Every ring was one of your Q10 picks.

From the inside out

  • core The settlement: the HQ and homes, built in tight clusters where neighbours boost each other (adjacency). People live here, and losing homes costs workers and income.
  • grid The power grid: nodes that feed turrets and factories. Cut a node and everything downstream goes dark. That makes it a target.
  • line Turret lines and bunkers: layered defence, strongest where the swarms come.
  • walls Walls and gates: funnel the swarms into killing zones; gates let your own army out.
  • border Claimed territory: the region's border. What you hold, you can build in.

Three more base ideas

Mobile bases, terraforming with a cost, and orbital support you have to earn.

07

Command: the army is the hero

No heroes, so big armies need good tools. Your four picks, from what you set once to what you do every second.

Formations

Lines, wedges and boxes that keep their shape on the move. Orbis already places units in slots; this gives the slots a shape.

Stances

Hold, attack, return fire, fall back when hurt. Set once, and a big army behaves without you.

Control groups

Number keys for groups, as in every RTS you've loved. Works at any zoom, even from space.

Rally and patrol

New units walk to the front by themselves; patrols guard the gaps between your regions.

08

The economy: mass, energy, people

Total Annihilation's flow, plus the settlement. Two resources and one number of people, so it stays simple.

Why this shape

  • Flow suits huge armies: you never save up for an army, you feed production.
  • Mass on rich ground pulls you outwards: the best deposits are past the frontier, often near ruins.
  • Energy runs through the grid, so defending power is defending everything.
  • People are one number: homes raise it, and it staffs extractors, factories and construction. No food chains, no happiness meters.
09

The tech spine

A shared trunk, then three branches. Each branch studies one face of the prize, has its own story line, and leads to its own ending.

Mixing branches: two, or all three?

Mixing is allowed and even rewarded, but never free, and never a way to have every ending at once. Round 4 chose four of the five limits.

Five ways to keep it in check

  • 1 · Different inputs (the natural limit). Material research runs on refined ore from your refineries; wildlife on specimens from the nests; ancients on artefacts from the ruins. You can't be everywhere, so what you spend your time on is your branch.
  • 2 · Opposed branches. Heavy mining raises agitation and breaks the bond, so material and wildlife pull against each other. Ancients works with either: its tools can exploit the planet or protect it.
  • 3 · Rising cost. Your second branch's research costs 50% more, your third 100% more. Generalists are possible, just slower.
  • 4 · Locked at the top. Starting an ending's T4 is a point of no return, a story moment, and the other two T4s close for good.
  • 5 · Rewards for mixing. Pairs unlock hybrids: ancient-alloy war machines (material + ancients), the ancients' living tech (wildlife + ancients). Material + wildlife: bio-weapons, if you can hold the bond.

Chosen in round 4: 1, 2, 4 and 5. Different inputs and the clash do most of the work without a rule the player has to learn; the lock at the top keeps the endings meaningful. Rising cost only if testing shows people still max out everything.

The tiers every branch climbs

T1 · Landing
  • walls, gates, basic turrets
  • extractors, generators, homes
  • light infantry and vehicles
T2 · Holding
  • bunkers, heavy turrets, artillery
  • shield generators
  • tanks, scouts that range far
T3 · From the ruins
  • missiles, long-range artillery
  • tech recovered from the lost civilisation
  • first experimentals
T4 · Reshape
  • nukes
  • giant experimentals
  • planet-shaping, by branch (below)

The unit and building lists are placeholders to show the shape; the real lists come later.

Planet-shaping, by branch

You picked all five powers in Q10, and that calling the megafauna belongs to the wildlife branch. A proposal for splitting the rest, so each branch reshapes the world in its own way:

Material

Engineer it

  • Raise and lower land: cut canyons, raise cliffs, fill valleys with heavy machines.
  • Move the sea: dams and pumps that flood lowlands or drain a basin.
Wildlife

Grow it

  • Call the megafauna: lure or summon the giants onto your enemy.
  • Change a region's climate: seed it until desert turns to jungle, or the ice spreads.
Ancients

Command it

  • Control the weather: wake the ancients' weather engines: send a storm, clear the sky.
  • Their reach: the ancients' machines can borrow a little of either other power, since they once commanded the whole planet.

Pushing back the dark

As the tech advances you hold back the night and the mist. Each step makes more of the map safe, and each has a catch so the dark never goes away for good.

The first slice: survive the swarm

A flat test map, ten waves of runners and brutes, a base you wall and power, and a handful of troops. Survive all ten; lose the HQ or your power and it's over.

WhereA flat test map; onto the planet once it plays well
You fight withWalls, gates, turrets and a few troops
The swarmRunners (fast, many) and brutes (slow, break walls)
WarningSensors you build, and you hear it first
WinSurvive N waves (ten to start)
LoseThe HQ falls, or the power is out too long
EconomyMass and energy as a flow; power grid
LookRealistic and gritty

Already in Orbis

  • A whole planet, ground-to-space camera, climate and deserts
  • Day and night clock, storms, rain, snow
  • Units at scale: 100 000 at 47 fps walking
  • Pathfinding with shared paths, formations, separation
  • Selection and move orders
  • Buildings placed on flattened ground (three placeholders)
  • Map editor, maps saved and loaded
  • Effects: particle bursts and converted effects (Atlas 1.5)

To build, in order

  1. A flat test map mode next to the planet
  2. Health, damage and death for units and buildings
  3. Buildings: HQ, wall, gate, turret, generator, extractor, sensor
  4. The power grid: generators feed turrets; an outage timer
  5. The flow economy: mass and energy, stalls when you overspend
  6. Turrets that pick targets and fire, with effects
  7. A few troops from the HQ, with stances
  8. Runners and brutes from nests; brutes go for walls
  9. A wave director: landing, ten waves, sound before sight, sensors
  10. Win and lose: ten waves held; HQ down or power out too long

Then: move it onto the planet. Later: homes and people, worse nights, the plants, one giant, one ruin.
