Orbis · concept draft 1 · from rounds 1 and 2

The planet fights back

You land on a living planet. You build a fortified settlement while the planet answers with swarms, giant creatures, storms and deadly nights. You spread across the globe one region at a time, digging up the ruins of a lost civilisation for the technology to strike back, until you can reshape the world itself. Then the other landings arrive.

00

Why are we here?

The reason has to explain why this planet and not an easier one. The strongest answer is that the planet is hard because it is rich: whatever makes it worth having is also what makes it fight.

Why this works in play, not just in the story

  • Every next region is a choice: safe and poor, or rich and angry. That is the campaign's risk and reward.
  • The danger has a cause you can learn and plan around, so it feels like a place rather than random spawns.
  • It explains the rival: once word of what you found gets out, someone else comes for it.
  • It sets up the ending: whatever made the planet rich and angry is what the lost civilisation left behind.

Four reasons to come, and why each picks the hard planet

The prize

Something here exists nowhere else: an exotic material, or the lost civilisation's technology. The company pays for the trip.

Why the hard planet: the easy ones have nothing worth the trip.

The exodus

Home is failing and this is the only world in reach of the ships.

Why the hard planet: there was no other choice.

The claim

A charter race: whoever holds the planet when the claim window closes owns it.

Why the hard planet: it's the one worth racing for, and the rival is coming.

The call

A signal from the ruins brought you here. Something wanted you to come.

Why the hard planet: you were invited, and you don't know why yet.

✓ Chosen Three layers of why

You picked this in chat. Each layer is true at a different point in the campaign. They fit your Q5 sides and the settlement you keep safe.

At the startWhat the company says: the prize. They fund the military landing to secure it. This is your briefing.
UnderneathWhat the colonists want: a way out. Home is failing and they signed on for a new one. The settlement is their home, not a mine, which is why keeping it safe matters.
Mid-campaignWhat the ruins reveal: the truth. The prize is what the lost civilisation was built on, and the planet's fury is connected to it: a defence they built, or the planet's answer to being mined. The company knew more than it said.

Why all four works

  • It's one cause, not four McGuffins. The core's energy rises and crystallises in the rock; the life that feeds on it grows huge; the lost civilisation built on it. Learn one, and the others make sense.
  • It explains every threat: storms (the energy), megafauna and swarms (the life), guardians (the lost tech), all strongest where the ground is richest.
  • It shapes the tech tree: the material, the life and the ruins are three branches, each with its own story, and all three end at the core, which is what lets you reshape the planet.
  • The life is studied, not sold. Specimens from fights and nests go to the lab, and each study pays off three ways (below).
  • It feeds the layers of why: the company sells the material; the truth in the ruins is what the core really is.
  • The one risk: if the four feel separate, the story blurs. Keep one name for the substance and show the link early.

Studying the life

Fighting a species teaches you how to fight it better. That is a reason to go into the nests, not just a cost.

02

The loop of play

Seven steps around a circle. Every lap the threats get bigger and so do your weapons.

What each step asks of you

  • Land and build: a landing period with nothing attacking. Place the settlement, the first walls, the power.
  • Flow: extractors bring in mass, generators energy, homes bring people. Spend faster than you earn and everything slows.
  • Scout: find the nests, the megafauna's paths, the ruins, the rich ground.
  • Take: clear a region's nests. That is where the frontier fighting happens.
  • Fortify: a region you fortify properly looks after itself (section 04).
  • Explore: dig the ruins for technology, story and caches. Digging may wake the guardians.
  • Tech up: new tiers bring bigger weapons, and the planet answers with bigger threats.
03

Who's on the planet, and when

One deep playable side. The threats arrive in order: the natives first, the machines when you dig, a rival when you're established.

04

One planet, region by region

Every region has a status. Your attention stays on the frontier; fortifying a region is how you earn the right to stop worrying about it.

How it ends: three ways to win ✓ Chosen

Round 3 Q5, from chat. Not escape. Each ending matches one of the three layers of why (section 00).

  • Dominate Hold and fortify every region. No special tech or understanding needed, just strength. The planet never stops fighting; you outlast it. The hardest fight.
  • Inherit Dig to the bottom of the ruins and face what was left there. Win, and its power is yours. Then choose: wipe out the natives, or hold them back for good.
  • Stabilise Calm the core instead of fighting it. "Make peace" felt wrong because you can't negotiate with an ecosystem. But the fury comes from the core's energy, and your mining stirs it up. Study the life, reach the core, and settle it: the swarms thin and the storms ease. You get there by bonding with the planet: your people and machines blend with its life until it stops treating you as an infection.

What makes Stabilise work: one meter

The planet's agitation. Mining, terraforming and digging raise it; it drives how big the waves and storms get. Dominate ignores it and fights through; Stabilise has to keep it down, which means mining less and holding more. The same meter also makes "richer ground, angrier planet" a rule you can see.

05

Anatomy of a base

Rings, from the people at the heart to the border you claim. Every ring was one of your Q10 picks.

From the inside out

  • core The settlement: the HQ and homes, built in tight clusters where neighbours boost each other (adjacency). People live here, and losing homes costs workers and income.
  • grid The power grid: nodes that feed turrets and factories. Cut a node and everything downstream goes dark. That makes it a target.
  • line Turret lines and bunkers: layered defence, strongest where the swarms come.
  • walls Walls and gates: funnel the swarms into killing zones; gates let your own army out.
  • border Claimed territory: the region's border. What you hold, you can build in.

Your three base ideas ✓ Chosen

Round 3 Q4, from chat: mobile bases, terraforming with a cost, and orbital support you have to earn.

06

The economy: mass, energy, people

Total Annihilation's flow, plus the settlement. Two resources and one number of people, so it stays simple.

Why this shape

  • Flow suits huge armies: you never save up for an army, you feed production.
  • Mass on rich ground pulls you outwards: the best deposits are past the frontier, often near ruins.
  • Energy runs through the grid, so defending power is defending everything.
  • People are one number: homes raise it, and it staffs extractors, factories and construction. No food chains, no happiness meters.
07

The tech spine

A shared trunk, then three branches. Each branch studies one face of the prize, has its own story line, and leads to its own ending.

The tiers every branch climbs

T1 · Landing
  • walls, gates, basic turrets
  • extractors, generators, homes
  • light infantry and vehicles
T2 · Holding
  • bunkers, heavy turrets, artillery
  • shield generators
  • tanks, scouts that range far
T3 · From the ruins
  • missiles, long-range artillery
  • tech recovered from the lost civilisation
  • first experimentals
T4 · Reshape
  • nukes
  • giant experimentals
  • planet-shaping: what exactly is round 3

The unit and building lists are placeholders to show the shape; the real lists come later.

Pushing back the dark ✓ Chosen

From chat: as the tech advances you hold back the night and the mist. Each step makes more of the map safe, and each has a catch so the dark never goes away for good.

09

First build: survive the swarm

One region, one night after another. Land, build, hold. Most of the hard engine work is done.

Already in Orbis

  • A whole planet, ground-to-space camera, climate and deserts
  • Day and night clock, storms, rain, snow
  • Units at scale: 100 000 at 47 fps walking
  • Pathfinding with shared paths, formations, separation
  • Selection and move orders
  • Buildings placed on flattened ground (three placeholders)
  • Fog of war (parked until the overlay maps on the planet)
  • Map editor, maps saved and loaded

To build, in order

  1. Health, damage and death for units and buildings
  2. Real buildings: wall, gate, turret, generator, extractor, home
  3. The flow economy: mass, energy, people, stalls
  4. Turrets that pick targets and fire
  5. Native swarms: spawn at nests, path to the base, attack what's in the way
  6. A wave director: landing period, announced waves, bigger at night
  7. Win and lose: hold until dawn of night N, or the HQ falls
