Digging in and living there

Two of your questions. How the material comes out of the ground, from surface seams to machines that reshape the planet. And where your people live: domes, or a city that grows by itself. My answer to both is a ladder: start small and gentle, and each step takes more from the planet and makes it angrier.

01

Getting the material out: a ladder

The substance crystallises in the rock. A little shows at the surface; the richest seams run deep, toward the core and the poles. Each rung digs deeper, yields more, and raises the region's irritation more (the lore page's agitation by region).

02

Reshaping the land

v1 has terraforming that costs a lot, raises agitation and only works near your structures. These machines are how it's done, and mining reshapes the land as a side effect.

The machines

  • Engineer vehicles (T1): flatten a build site, fill a crater, cut a ramp. Small jobs, near your base.
  • Earthmovers (T2): raise a ridge or dig a trench or moat. A moat filled with fire is the weapons page's burning moat.
  • Terraform towers (T3, material): reshape a whole area around them over minutes: lift a plateau to build a fortress on, sink a valley to funnel a wave.
  • Planet-shaping (T4, v1): material moves the sea and raises mountains; wildlife grows the land (forests, climate); ancients command the weather.

Why it's good for a turtle

  • Shape the approach. A ridge with one gap is a free wall line; a moat stops runners and brutes alike (burrowers and flyers don't care, as in v1).
  • Strip mines become defences. An old open pit is a ditch the swarm has to climb out of under your guns.
  • The catch: it's slow and costly, it raises irritation, and the planet answers terraforming with burrowers under, flyers over and giants through (v1).

Can Orbis do it?

Yes. The land is already editable in the editor, and pathfinding re-routes when the ground changes (P5: a ridge raised under a moving army). What it can't do is tunnels or overhangs: the land is a heightfield, so mines are open pits, and deep drills go down a shaft we fake (like the pits).

03

Where your people live

Three ways to do it. They're not exclusive: my pick is the third, which takes the best of the other two.

A

Domes

Sealed habitats, because the air outside isn't safe (spores, the mist). You place each dome; it holds a set number of people.

Good: the iconic frontier image. A clear thing to protect. Glowing domes read from orbit at night.
Less good: static; a base of identical bubbles.

B

A city that grows

You zone areas (homes, work, farms) and the town fills in by itself as people arrive, the way They Are Billions' tents become houses.

Good: a living city, little micro, and it looks different every game.
Less good: hard to defend if it sprawls; no strong image.

Cmy pick

Domes that grow towns

You build the domes, a big investment and the heart of each settlement. Inside and around them the town grows by itself as people and comfort rise: homes, lights, streets, gardens, tubes between domes.

Good: the image of A and the life of B. You decide where the domes go (defence), the town decides how it looks.

04

How a settlement grows

What you place, what grows

  • You place: domes, the power grid, walls, turrets, extractors, factories, labs. The big decisions.
  • It grows: homes, streets, lights, gardens, markets, inside and around the domes, as people arrive and comfort rises. Little to manage.
  • Comfort comes from v1's adjacency bonuses: power, safety (turrets near), light, gardens, a lab or a church nearby. Comfortable domes grow faster and send more people to work.

When a dome breaks

  • A breached dome loses its air: people evacuate to the next dome along the tubes, or are lost.
  • The Given take the people they reach. They may come back in a later wave (the lore page's "your dead come back"), so a breach costs twice.
  • That's why the domes sit inside the walls, and why the turtle builds outward from them.

Can Orbis draw domes?

The shapes are easy. Glass that you see the town through needs a transparent material in Atlas, which is an engine question for the Atlas chat; until then the domes can be frosted, lit from inside, or lattice-framed.

06

People in the economy

v1's economy is mass and energy as a flow, plus people. This is what people would do.

Crews

Extractors, miners, drills and factories need crews to run. More people, more of your economy running at once.

Soldiers and scientists

Troops and research come out of the population: you can't field an army and staff every lab at once.

The reason to hold

The colonists are why you're here (v1: a way out, not a mine). Losing people costs you economy, and it's the story's real loss.
