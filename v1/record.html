Round 2: the concept builder

Q1. Which direction?
All three, in layers
Q2. Who is the main enemy?
Natives first, factions later
Q3. What does the planet throw at you?
Swarms; giant megafauna; hostile weather; dangerous nights; rogue machines
Q4. How many playable factions to start with?
One, deep
Q5. Which factions land on the planet?
Human military; corporate colonists; machine intelligence
Q6. How should the early game protect you?
A landing period; announced first waves; cheap, strong defence
Q7. What sits at the top of the tech tree?
Planet-shaping; nukes and missiles; giant experimentals
Q8. Which economy?
Mass and energy as a flow
Q9. Units you care about?
Neither: the army is the hero
Q10. What makes a base yours?
Walls and gates; turret lines; territory; power grid; adjacency; settlement; my own idea (answered in round 3)
Q11. What shape is the campaign?
One persistent planet, region by region Your note in chat: one long persistent campaign, region by region, with exploration and the remains of lost civilisations.
Q12. What does exploring the planet turn up?
Lost technology; sleeping guardians; the planet's story; caches; clues to the natives
Q13. What happens to regions you've already secured?
Combined: a status per region, with surges
Q14. How long is a mission or match?
Varies by mission
Q15. What should we build first?
Survive the swarm
3

Round 3: filling the gaps

Q1. Is this the right cast?
Yes, as drafted
Q2. Why are we here?
Layered, as drafted
Q3. If there's a prize, what is it?
Energy from the core; an exotic material; the lost technology; studying the life
Q4. Your own idea for the base
Mobile bases; terraforming the base ground; orbital support Mobile bases crawl out to a new area and unfold as an outpost to hold it, widen the border, or stay a satellite. Terraforming costs enough that you can't go crazy with it. Orbital support only after you clear the ground and the mist that blocks orbit; storms cut it off, so you can't rely on it all the time.
Q5. How does the campaign end?
Several endings; wake what was buried; stabilise the planet; take the whole planet Each ending has its own tech branch and story line: material leads to Dominate, wildlife to Stabilise, ancients to Inherit. Shared trunk; dip into any branch, go deep in one to end the campaign.
Q6. What are the natives, really?
The planet is alive
Q7. No heroes: how do you steer a big army?
Stances; control groups; rally and patrol routes; formations
Q8. How do the giant creatures behave?
Guardians of places; hunters that roam; can be lured or used Not just sandworms: big creatures of different species, and even plant life.
Q9. How long is a day?
Set by the region As tech advances you push back the dark and the mist: floodlights and flares, mist scrubbers, an uplink network, orbital mirrors. Each has a catch.
Q10. What does planet-shaping mean?
Control the weather; change a region's climate; raise and lower land; move the sea; call the megafauna Calling the megafauna belongs to the wildlife tech tree.
Q11. What's the tone?
All four, in order The opening is C&C energy (not big personalities: heavy metal, aggressive building, pumped) and grim survival against the odds, driven by the dream of a heroic frontier. Later it splits by branch: a mystery to uncover (ancients), becoming part of the planet and bonding with it (wildlife), or a louder war (material).
Q12. What goes into the first playable slice?
Walls and gates; turrets that fire; power grid; mass and energy flow; announced waves
Q13. Keep the name Orbis?
Keep Orbis

Decided in conversation

Ideas you raised in chat between rounds 3 and 4, and what they became.

Why this planet?
It's hard because it's rich: three layers of why (the company's prize, the colonists' way out, the truth in the ruins)
The prize
All four faces as one substance; the life is studied, not sold: to understand the material, tune weapons, and learn what the natives are
Base ideas
Crawler outposts; terraforming with real costs (energy, time, the planet notices, the natives answer it); orbital support earned by clearing the mist and cut by storms
Endings
Three ways to win: Dominate, Stabilise, Inherit (then purge the natives or hold them back); not escape
Pushing back the dark
Tech holds back the night and the mist: floodlights, mist scrubbers, an uplink network, orbital mirrors
Three tech branches
Material (refine, build weapons), wildlife (study and capture creatures), ancients (dig up and study the lost civilisation), each with its own story and ending
Tone
Pumped and grim to start, then split by branch
The poles
The richest rewards and the harshest world: the dark lets the planet flourish there; land at the equator, push toward a pole
The Stabilise twist
Bonding means mining less; the company comes for its money, and you and the planet defend it together
Mixing branches
Asked how two or three branches would be controlled; five limits proposed, chosen in round 4
