Orbis · lore round 1 · towards concept v2

The Given

The things pouring out of the nests were people once. The ancients weren't born here: they came, as you have. They fought the planet's own creatures, then made a pact with it and fed it their own to keep it quiet. They gave too many, the planet took the rest, and it kept them all. This page tells that story, shows where it could go another way, and asks you to pick. Take what works and leave the rest: nothing here changes v1 until you say so.

Revised after your note: the ancients came from elsewhere and were absorbed, and the planet has its own creatures too. That splits the enemy into two lineages, one per branch (section 04).

Decided: lore round 1, answered

Every answer, in short. The full record with your notes is in answers-lore1.md next to this page. This goes into concept v2, together with the weapons, settlement and environment rounds.

The story

  • The Given are the heart of the lore, but you only learn it by studying (Q1). The calm-the-planet path finds out in time; the others only suspect; the ancients' path finds out too late, and regrets it.
  • The ancients came from elsewhere, like you (Q2), but the ruins look native until deep study finds their landing field, ship and star charts. The company's survey calls them "the planet's first people": convenient, since a dead native people leaves no one to answer to.
  • They were human-ish, about 2.5 m tall and slender, with long hands.
  • The tithe was a slide into darkness (Q4, Q5, Q6). It started to end the war, became a trade for the core's power, and ended as worship. Volunteers led by their own leaders, then the condemned, then a lottery, then a caste bred for it. A generation tried to stop; the Given came up and took most of them; the last walked down willingly.
  • The heart is a place, a being and the Eldest's voice (Q10). On the Inherit path no rival lands: you end against the Eldest (Q7). The endings stand as written (Q22).
  • The reveal goes horror, then tragedy, then awe (Q23). The name is the Given (Q24).

The creatures

  • Two lineages, one per branch (Q3). The Given are runners, spitters and the Eldest (Q9). The planet's own life is the brutes, burrowers, flyers, giants and megaflora.
  • Beasts first: the Given stay underground until the heart's first threshold, then break out. Before that, only glimpses at the edge of your lights.
  • How much of the person is left: it varies by kind, and rare Echoes remember more (Q8).
  • The look (Q11): two postures, the ancients' build stretched, no eyes, glowing seams of the substance, relics, wet from below.
  • In play (Q12): calls that are almost words, nests are tithe shafts, stronger at night, blind hunters drawn by noise, Echoes.
  • Guardians (Q17, Q18): wall guardians, shaft keepers and the heart's guard, with every rule kept.

The world's systems

  • Pits (Q13, Q14): the Given's nests, in three sizes. They grow, new breaches open, and mist rises from them. You can seal, tap, descend or calm them. Not chosen: pits linked underground.
  • Agitation by region plus the heart (Q15, Q16), with every rule: regions cool when quiet, tunnels spread irritation between neighbours, the heart never drains, its thresholds are the big beats, orbit shows a heat map, and each branch treats it differently.

Choices

  • The tithe is offered to you before the truth (Q19).
  • All six other choices are in (Q20): an Echo, a guardian, a captured Given, tapping the great pit, telling the colonists, and the company wanting your dead.
  • How choices come (Q21): from advisors who can be wrong, waiting on screen without pausing the game, and kept in a record of choices where later consequences are added to earlier entries.

What the story has to fit

Concept v1 already fixes a few things. The idea fits all of them. Several it explains, where v1 had only placeholders.

Fixed in v1

  • The planet is one living thing; everything that attacks you is its immune response
  • One agitation meter: mining, digging and terraforming make it answer harder
  • The prize: one substance with four faces (core energy, the material, the life, the ancients' tech)
  • A lost civilisation, the ancients, whose ruins hold tech, story and sleeping machine guardians
  • Three endings: Dominate, Stabilise (bond with the planet), Inherit (take the ancients' power, then purge the natives or hold them back)
  • Exploring turns up "clues to what the natives are"
  • The rival on the Inherit path: "whatever drove the ancients out"

What v1 left open, and this answers

  • What the swarm is. v1 says "the planet's immune system" but not where its creatures come from. Here: they're the ancients' own people, changed.
  • Why the ruins matter to the fight. The ruins are the record of how it happened. The nests sit on the places it happened.
  • What "purge or hold back the natives" really costs. It's a far heavier choice if the natives were people.
  • How the ancients lived with the planet. v1's tech map says the ancients "combine with either" branch and once tried to command the planet. The tithe was how.
  • Who the ancients were. Colonists before you. Their ruins are a colony like yours: walls, a grid, homes. You're repeating their story.
  • Why two research branches study the planet's creatures. There are two lineages: the planet's own life (wildlife) and the Given (ancients). Section 04.
  • What drove the ancients out (Inherit's rival). Nothing did: they were swallowed. The rival is whoever sent them (section 03).
01

Your idea

Most of the fodder creatures, the runners, could be what the natives evolved into after some story around the ancients. They gave parts of their population to the planet; that group was changed and merged with the living planet, and over time became more vicious, monster-like creatures, connected to the heart of the planet.

Then: the ancients could be a civilisation that came to the planet as well, not born here. They got absorbed and lost themselves to it after sacrificing too many of the Given, and were overwhelmed. The planet has its own creature inhabitants too, which they fought. Is that a good division between the ancients path and the nature-blend path?

Your three reference stills set the mood:

  • gaunt, hunched bodies with long limbs and long fingers;
  • pale grey skin pulled over bone;
  • sunken or missing eyes, and a wide jaw;
  • wet, dark places in a cold teal light.
We take those traits and draw our own creature from them, not a copy of the stills. Section 06 turns the traits into the story.

02

The story, as I'd tell it

Five ages, told from the ancients' side: colonists who came before you, fought the planet, made a pact with it, and were swallowed. The player learns it backwards, from the ruins and from the creatures themselves (section 09).

age 1

They arrive

The ancients aren't from here. They come from another world, for the same reason you do: the substance. Its energy rises from the core and crystallises in the rock. They land, build a colony and then cities, all running on it: light, machines, the weather engines. Their ruins are a colony like yours, with walls, a grid and homes. The player's story is a rhyme of this one.

What they were like: human-ish, but not human. They were taller than us, around two and a half metres, and slender, with long limbs, long hands and narrow faces. Their doorways, stairs and statues are built to that size, so the ruins feel a little too tall for your people from the first one you enter.

age 2

War with the planet

They dig toward the poles, where the energy runs richest, and the planet answers. Its own creatures (beasts, giants, the creeping forest) come at the walls. Storms come and the nights grow long. Their machines hold the line, but it costs them every year. The guardians in the ruins are the war machines they built for this.

age 3

The pact

Someone finds a way to end the war, or thinks they do. The planet doesn't want the machines or the mines. It wants something living to take in. A procession walks down the deep shafts to where the core's energy pools, the place the ancients call the heart. They don't come back. The beasts draw off, and the planet goes quiet for a generation. The ancients call the ones who went the Given.

age 4

Too many given

Who went down, in order:
  • Volunteers. Honoured, led by the ancients' own leaders: the first procession was heroes.
  • The condemned. When the volunteers ran out, the prisons emptied downward, and the laws grew to keep them full.
  • A lottery. Anyone, every year: carved name lists in the halls, tokens round the Given's necks.
  • A caste bred for it. The leaders' descendants never went themselves; they bred people to go instead. Nurseries stand by the shafts, rows of cradles already promised to the heart.

The tithe becomes a law, a festival, then a terror. Each generation walks down more people, because every time they dig deeper the planet asks for more, and they never stop digging. Down in the heart the Given don't die: the core's energy remakes them into the planet's own flesh, and they breed there in the dark.

  • The first generations still look like their people (tall, slender, long-handed), only paler, thinner and blind.
  • Later ones lose the rest: longer limbs, a mouth for a face, the substance growing through them like bone.

The planet has learned something it didn't know before: how to turn invaders into its own. Its beasts were born to it. The Given were made from the ancients, and they understand walls, gates and people. That makes them far more dangerous.

age 5

Overwhelmed

One day the tithe isn't enough. It may come when a generation tries to stop, or when the Given outnumber the givers (section 03). The Given come up the shafts in the thousands, and the planet takes the rest of the ancients: not killed, absorbed. Their cities go quiet. Bred for countless generations since, the Given are the swarm the planet sends at anything that digs too deep. The nests are the old tithe shafts, still open to the heart.

now

You land

The planet has been quiet a long time, and quiet is why the company's survey called it safe. Your drills reach the substance and the planet answers the way it first answered the ancients: its own beasts come out of the wild. For a while that's the whole war. Then, as the heart stirs, something else starts to move below. Tall shapes stand at the edge of your lights at night and are gone when you look again. Then the ground opens, and the Given come up, some of them still wearing the ancients' shapes.

The forks: what tipped it, and who comes back

Your version settles the ending: they gave too many and were overwhelmed. Two questions are still open. What tipped them over? And since they came from elsewhere, does anyone out there know what happened? The second one decides Inherit's rival.

What tipped it

Amy pick

They tried to stop

A generation refused to give. Within a season the Given came up the shafts and took everyone. The planet never wanted an offering; it wanted all of them, and the tithe only kept it waiting.

Why it works: it's a warning to you. It gives Stabilise its stakes: can you bond without feeding it?

B

The Given outnumbered them

No one decided anything. The tithe grew faster than the colony, until there were more Given below than people above, and the balance tipped by itself.

Why it works: slow and inevitable, and it mirrors your own agitation meter: dig more, give more, until it's too late.

C

The last walked in willingly

By the end the heart was a religion. The last of the ancients walked down believing it was a better life. The ruins are empty because the people chose to go.

Why it works: the most unsettling. It makes Stabilise very dark: is bonding the same choice they made?

The ancients came from somewhere. Their home world lost contact long ago. When your digging wakes the ancients' beacon, their own kind come back to claim what their colony found.

Why it works: it follows directly from your note. You're doing to the ancients' heirs what the company would do to you.

A few refused the tithe and fled before the end. They're still out there, and they've had a long time to decide what they want from the heart.

Why it works: the survivors' guilt and grief make a rival with a motive, not just an army.

No one's coming. The last enemy on the Inherit path is the Eldest at the heart: the first Given, the ancients' own leaders, absorbed and speaking for the planet.

Why it works: no second faction to build. The cheapest option, and still a strong end.

04

Two lineages, two branches

Yes, it's a good division, and a cleaner one than v1 had. The planet fights with two kinds of creature from two sources. Each research branch studies one of them, and each ending answers it.

Why it works

  • Two different enemies to read. Beasts are animals: they follow territory, noise and hunger. The Given were people: they find the gate, go for the generators, and come at night. You learn to tell them apart by how they attack.
  • Each branch has its own subject. Wildlife takes specimens from beasts and their dens; ancients takes artefacts from ruins and relics from the Given. The two never compete for the same input (v1's mixing rule 1).
  • The ancients sit on both sides. They fought the planet's own life (their war machines are the guardians) and then became the Given. That's why the ancients branch combines with either other one.
  • Stabilise gets a clear meaning. The ancients never bonded with the planet's own life; they fought it, then bought peace with people. Bonding with the native life is the third way they never tried.
  • The planet learns. It made the Given from the last invaders, and it will try with you. That's what makes "your dead come back" (section 07) logical, not just horror.

Watch out for

  • Two rosters cost twice the art. The slice has two kinds; v1 adds giants, megaflora, spitters, burrowers and flyers. Splitting them between the lineages costs nothing extra if each kind belongs to exactly one.
  • Where the Given live versus the beasts. Nests are tithe shafts (Given). Beasts need homes of their own: dens, hives, the creeping forest. That matters for mixing too: wildlife specimens come from dens, not nests.
  • The slice. Beasts first (your call): the slice's fodder is a native swarm beast and its tanks are brutes, from dens and hives. The crawler model Orbis uses today already looks like a beast, so it fits. The Given and the pits arrive later, at the heart's first threshold.

05

The pits: holes down to the heart

Your idea fits the story exactly. The nests are pits: the shafts the ancients dug for the tithe. Some are shallow, and the great ones go all the way down to the heart. The Given pour out of them. The planet's own beasts don't use them; they come from dens on the surface.

Three sizes

  • breach Breaches: fresh holes the planet tears open when agitation peaks, sometimes close to your walls. The ground shakes and cracks first, so you get a warning.
  • pit Pits: the nests. Old tithe shafts with the ancients' carved rims and fallen scaffolding. Each one feeds a region's waves.
  • great pit Great pits: few, near the poles, all the way to the heart. Ruins line their walls. This is the endgame.

How they look

  • A black mouth in the ground with a carved rim, and broken ancient scaffolding leaning over it.
  • A faint glow far below: the core's colour, the same as the glow on the Given.
  • Warm air and mist rising, thick at night. The mist that blocks orbit comes out of the pits.
  • Sound before sight: the calls echo up the shaft before anything climbs out.

What you can do with one

  • Seal it: kill what's at the mouth and cap it with a seal. It holds while it's powered; if the power fails, it bursts.
  • Tap it (material): a core rig over the shaft gives huge energy, and agitation spikes.
  • Descend (ancients): send an expedition down to the ruin in the shaft wall.
  • Calm it (wildlife, late): once you've bonded with the planet, a pit can be quieted without a seal. It's the Stabilise way of closing one.

Rules that make them interesting

  • They're connected. Tunnels link the pits underground. Seal one and the swarm comes out of the next one over, so sealing is strategy, not just clean-up.
  • They grow. A pit left alone gets wider and sends bigger waves. One near your base is a clock.
  • New ones open. High agitation tears new breaches, which is the turtle's worst fear. You read the ground (cracks, tremors, sensors) to see them coming.
  • Fog of war hides them, but the mist over them shows from orbit and from the air: a column of mist is a pit.

Can Orbis draw a hole to the core?

Not a real one: the land is a heightfield on a sphere, so it can't have holes or overhangs. But from an RTS camera it doesn't need them:

  • a steep crater in the heightfield;
  • a model for the mouth: the carved rim, the scaffolding, and a black shaft disc that hides the bottom;
  • a point light deep in the crater for the glow, and an Atlas mist volume over it (we have both);
  • the waves spawn at the rim.

Great pits need the same, bigger, plus a cutaway view or a mission underground if we ever go down. The slice's two nests (boxes today) would become pits first.

06

The look: the story on their bodies

Your reference stills' mood (gaunt, pale, blind, wet dark, cold teal light) is right for the runners. A few rules make the backstory readable without a word of text.

Two postures: low to run, hunched to stand

  • One body, two ways of moving. The Given run on all fours, fast and low with their long arms reaching forward. When they stop they rise into a hunched, gaunt, human-like stance: head forward, arms hanging to their knees.
  • The posture tells you what it's doing.
    • A wave coming in low is charging.
    • Given standing still at the edge of your lights are listening. Their heads tilt, because they're blind.
    • One that rises at your wall is about to strike or climb.
    You can read it from the RTS camera by their height.
  • The last trace of the person. When they stop, they stand the way the ancients stood, and that's the uncanny moment. The older the Given, the straighter it stands: the Eldest are almost upright, at the ancients' full height, taller than anything human.
  • For the models: one skeleton for runners and spitters, with these clips:
    • a four-legged run;
    • a hunched idle with listening head-tilts;
    • rise and drop transitions;
    • a standing strike, and a lunge from all fours.
    Atlas's animation at a distance means only the near ones pay for the detail.

Rules for the Given

  • The ancients' build, stretched further. The ancients were already human-ish, tall (about 2.5 m) and slender. The Given keep that frame and the change pulls it further: runners have arms to the ground and fingers like tools. Standing hunched, a runner is still about as tall as one of your soldiers. Upright, it would tower over them.
  • No eyes, or eyes grown over. They lived generations in the dark and hunt by sound and vibration. That explains why noise draws them (v1: giants follow noise; now the swarm does too).
  • The substance shows through. Crystal seams in the skin glowing faintly the colour of the core's energy: dim on runners, brighter on spitters, brightest on the Eldest. You can read how deep a creature has been from how much it glows.
  • Relics. Now and then one carries something from its past: a band of the ancients' metal sunk into a wrist, a glyph scarred into its back that matches the ruins. Rare, so finding one means something.
  • Wet, fresh from below. Slick skin, steam at night, a trail where a swarm has passed.

The planet's own life looks different

  • Animals, not people. Six legs, carapace, hide, horn: nothing human about them, so the two lineages read apart at a glance.
  • Eyes and colour. They see, they're patterned like living things, and they come out by day as much as night.
  • No glow, or only at the joints. The glow belongs to the Given, who were made in the heart.

What it means for the models

  • The runner model changes from the current crawler to the two-posture Given above. It's readable at RTS distance by its posture: low when it moves, hunched and taller when it stops.
  • A faint emissive in crystal seams: the clumps' dots and far LODs can carry the glow, so a swarm at night reads as moving cold light.
  • The swarm's red tint today becomes the core's colour, on the Given only. The beasts keep natural colours, so a mixed wave reads as two kinds of thing.
  • Brutes become a native beast: low, armoured, a battering head. The current Karcinomorph model already fits that better than a person would.
  • Sound before sight: wet movement, clicking, and the broken calls.
07

How it plays

Lore earns its place when it changes what you do. Pick the ones you like; each stands on its own.

  • sound Calls that are almost words. The wave you hear before you see it (slice step 9) is a chorus of broken voices. Late in the archaeology branch your people start to make them out: names, and one word over and over.
  • nests Nests are tithe shafts. Killing a nest uncovers the shaft under it, which is the way into a ruin. Clearing the swarm and digging up the past become the same act, and it raises agitation.
  • night They were made in the dark. Stronger and bolder at night (v1's long nights); light hurts them but draws them (v1: floodlights draw the night swarm).
  • noise Blind hunters. Heavy industry, artillery and drilling draw them; quiet bases are attacked less. It's a real reason to turtle quietly.
  • two enemies Beasts and Given attack differently. Beasts go for whatever is nearest and loudest, and scatter when hurt. The Given go for the gate, the generators and the homes, and never break. Mixed waves make you defend against both.
  • echoes Echoes. Rarely, a Given that remembers: it doesn't attack, it walks somewhere. Follow it and it leads to a ruin, a cache or a shaft. Shoot it and you'll never know. It's a choice the material branch never thinks twice about.
  • your dead The planet takes your dead (optional, dark). Colonists lost outside the walls can come back as Given in a later wave, the way the infected do in They Are Billions. It's grim, but it makes keeping people safe matter.
  • study Two labs, two stories. The wildlife lab studies the beasts from their dens: how the planet's own life works, and how to bond with it. The ancients' archaeologists study relics taken off the Given, and their first finding is "these were people".
  • dens Beasts have dens, the Given have shafts. Clearing a den gives specimens; clearing a shaft opens a ruin.
  • heart The heart is a place. Under the pole: the deepest ruin, the Eldest, and where every ending is decided.

Agitation by region, and the heart's

Your note: one meter for the whole planet makes everywhere matter all the time. Instead, two layers: each region has its own irritation, and the heart's agitation builds up from all of them over the campaign. Orbit shows you where you're needed, and the ground is where you answer it.

A region's irritation

  • Raised by what you do there: drilling and mining most, then heavy industry, artillery and noise, terraforming, digging into ruins, killing at the pits.
  • Sets that region's waves: how big, how often, which kinds.
  • Cools when you're quiet, but never below the heart's level.
  • Leaks to neighbours through the pits' tunnels, unless you seal them.

The heart's agitation

  • Fills from every region, a share of each one's irritation, plus every great pit you tap or descend.
  • Never really drains. Only the Stabilise path can bring it down, and that's what the path is for.
  • Raises the floor everywhere, and its thresholds are the campaign's big beats: new kinds, storms and surges, great breaches, the heart waking.
  • It's the campaign clock. The more you take, the sooner the end comes.

Close up and from afar

  • From orbit: the planet as a heat map. Regions glow with irritation, mist columns mark active pits, and warnings come up where a wave is building.
  • On the ground: fly down to the region that needs you and hold its line. The rest simmer meanwhile.
  • Choosing where to be is the strategy: calm a region by leaving it, or fortify it so it holds without you (v1's region statuses).
  • Planet-wide surges (v1) are the moments the heart crosses a threshold: everywhere at once.

The guardians: machines still following orders

v1's sleeping machine guardians stay, and the new story gives them a reason. The ancients built them to fight the planet's own creatures, and some later to keep order at the tithe. Their makers are gone, but the orders aren't.

war machines

Wall guardians

Built in the war with the beasts to hold the colony. They sleep in the ruins' walls and gates, and wake when anything comes in: you, the beasts, even the Given. To them every intruder is the enemy they were made for.

keepers

Shaft keepers

Built to escort the tithe processions down the pits. They still stand at the shafts, keeping order at a ritual no one performs any more. They let the Given pass, because the Given are the procession. They stop anything else from going down.

the deep

The heart's guard

The largest, at the deepest ruins near the poles, set to guard the heart itself. They're the Inherit path's last wall before the Eldest: a boss fight for the material path too, if it digs that deep.

What makes them interesting

  • They don't know their makers are gone. A guardian sees the Given as its own people and won't fire on them. So at the deepest ruins, guardians and Given stand side by side: grim, and a puzzle for you.
  • Waking one is a gamble. What it does depends on the orders it woke with. A wall guardian may fight the swarm next to you, or turn on you because you're in its ruin.
  • They fight the beasts on sight. That's the war they were built for, so a guardian woken near a beast den draws the beasts off your walls.
  • They carry the story. Their memory holds the ancients' orders, logs and the tithe's schedule: the ancients branch's best source after the murals.

In play they're the weapons page's "machine" class: weak to lightning and the ancients' tech, strong against fire and natural weapons.

Choices along the way

Your idea: as your studies go on beside the fighting and expanding, they turn up choices. The strongest one is the tithe itself, offered as a sensible answer before you know what the Given are. The player can walk the ancients' road without knowing it, and find out later.

the big one

The tithe offer

  • When: mid-campaign. Your archaeologists translate the first records in a ruin while a region is being crushed by waves.
  • The pitch: "The ancients ended their war by sending volunteers down to the heart, and the planet went quiet." There are volunteers among your colonists who believe it's an honour, maybe even a way home.
  • If you accept, it works. That region's irritation drops hard and the waves stop for a long while. It's a real, tempting payoff, the same deal the ancients got.
  • The price comes later. The planet asks for more next time, and the heart's agitation remembers.
  • The reveal. In a later wave you see Given wearing your colony's suits and insignia. That's how the player learns what the Given are, and that they made some.
  • It touches every ending. On Stabilise, tithing breaks the bond. On Inherit, "hold them back" is the tithe, so you've already started. On Dominate, the company pushes for it, because it's cheaper than turrets.

Other choices your studies turn up

The rule for all of them: offered with only part of the truth, each reasonable when you make it, paid for or rewarded later. The game never labels them good or evil.

How choices show up in play

  • A message from the branch's people (the engineers, the scientists, the archaeologists), with the facts they have and their advice. Your advisors can be wrong.
  • The choice waits a while, so you can finish a fight. It doesn't pause the game.
  • Every choice is remembered on a short record you can read: what you chose, and later what it led to.

Why it fits Orbis

  • It makes the story something you do, not something you read: you can repeat the ancients' mistake with your own hands.
  • It ties the three systems together: studies (research), fighting (agitation) and the colonists (people in the economy).
  • It's cheap to build: a message, a choice and a flag, with the payoff in things the game already has (waves, irritation, units).
