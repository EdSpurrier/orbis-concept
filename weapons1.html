Orbis · weapons round 1 · towards concept v2

What you fight with

Nine weapon families, from your list. Each is good against some of what the planet sends and bad against the rest. Each costs something different: mass, power, noise, or the planet's patience. Each comes from a different part of the tech tree. The point is that no single weapon holds a wall, so a base is a mix, and the mix you choose says which path you're on.

00

The shape of it

Three ideas carry the whole page. Together they turn weapons into choices rather than upgrades.

1 · Soft counters

Every family does extra or reduced damage against each kind of target (section 01). Nothing is useless, but the right weapon does two or three times the work. A wall of flamers melts a runner swarm and barely scratches a brute. That's the Total Annihilation and They Are Billions feel: you read the wave and build the answer.

2 · Different costs

Guns and explosives cost mass. Energy weapons draw power from the grid every shot, so a big fight can brown out your base. Loud weapons raise the region's irritation, the lore page's agitation by region. Material weapons cost the planet's patience: the substance itself draws the Given.

3 · Paths have signatures

The trunk gives everyone guns, explosives and fire. Then each branch adds its own families: material gets plasma, the substance's own weapons and the big explosives; wildlife gets natural weapons; ancients gets the ancients' tech. A late base shows its path in its turret line.

06

Weapons and the world

Orbis already has weather, day and night, forest, mist and a living planet. Weapons should feel it.

Weather and terrain

  • Rain puts out fire and shortens it; it makes lightning jump further (wet ground, wet bodies).
  • Mist and dust cut laser range (the light scatters); guns and explosives don't care.
  • Forest burns. Fire spreads into megaflora: a weapon against the creeping forest, and a danger to your own outposts in the trees.
  • Explosives scar the land: craters slow units. The biggest blasts reshape a ridge (material's terraforming, by force).
  • Night: muzzle flashes and fire light the dark and draw the Given (they hunt by sound, and light draws the night swarm, as in v1).

The look and sound (what Orbis has already)

  • The effects library Orbis plays from (978 converted effects in Atlas) already has fire, explosions, lightning impacts, sparks and blood. Fire, explosives and lightning can look right early.
  • Tracers and muzzle flashes are in today; beams (lasers) need a beam effect, and the player supports them.
  • Each family gets its own sound: the dull thud of mortars, the crack of lightning, the hiss of plasma, the silence of a laser.
  • Effects are budgeted per kind, so a battle of thousands stays fast (the combat fix earlier today).
